1

Why was the law needed?

The problem → the purpose → the protection

The problem

Low & unequal wages

The book describes wages as low and different across regions, with no established minimum wage and weak bargaining power for workers.

The need

Protection from exploitation

Workers needed a legal floor below which employers should not pay for covered employment.

Social objective

Reduce poverty

Provide a basic standard of living and protect workers from exploitation.

Economic objective

Encourage productive work

A suitable wage should encourage workers to put in effort and support improvement in the economy and living standards.

Scope & objectives — remember the action words

  1. Fix minimum wage rates and revise them periodically (the text says every five years).
  2. Secure adequate living wages in the public interest.
  3. Fix daily working hours.
  4. Prevent exploitation by employers.
  5. Ensure a decent standard of living.
  6. Provide basic physical needs, good health and comfort.
  7. Penalise employers who fail to pay minimum wages.
  8. Establish advisory boards and inspection arrangements.
  9. Lay down inspector powers and duties.
  10. Prevent wrongful infringement of workers' rights.
  11. Provide authorities for claims/relief when wages are not paid.
  12. Authorise Central and State Governments to make rules.
2

What does “minimum wage” mean?

A wage floor, not a reward for exceptional performance

ILO definition — simplified

The minimum amount of remuneration an employer is required to pay for work performed during a given period; it cannot be reduced by collective agreement or individual contract.

Easy memory: Minimum wage = the lowest legally required payment for the work covered.
Purpose 1

Protect workers

Prevent undue low pay and ensure adequate wages for work performed.

Purpose 2

Reduce poverty

Help maintain a minimum living standard and reduce discrimination in remuneration.

Purpose 3

Support employment policy

Complement collective bargaining and other social/employment policies.

Coverage idea

Many job categories

The book notes India’s complex wage-setting system across many employments and job categories.

3

Three wage concepts

Use this comparison to avoid mixing them up

Minimum wage

Basic floor

Wage intended to cover bare subsistence/basic needs and a minimum level of comfort.

Memory cue: “Minimum = floor.”

Fair wage

Above the minimum

The book describes fair wage as more than minimum wage; it seeks to maintain employment and considers the industry’s capacity to pay adequate remuneration.

Memory cue: “Fair = balance.”

Living wage

Broader human needs

Supports adequate shelter, food, necessities, health, sanitation, education, dignity, comfort and contingencies.

Memory cue: “Living = dignified life.”

The book links the three concepts to the Fair Wage Committee and its wage-policy framework.

How are minimum wages fixed?

Section 3 → appropriate government → rate and revision

Appropriate Government
→
Fix / revise rates
→
For scheduled employment

According to the supplied text, Section 3 empowers the appropriate government (Central or State, as applicable) to fix minimum wages. Rates may be expressed by hour, day, month or another prescribed wage period.

Committee method

Committees/subcommittees examine the matter and make recommendations.

Notification method

Government publishes proposals/notification and follows the prescribed process.

Revision

The text states that rates are to be revised periodically, and mentions a five-year interval in its objectives.

Different rates may exist

Rates need not be uniform: they may vary by area, employment, work or other relevant criteria described in the text.

Rate structures — visual comparison

Time rate ⏱

Payment is based on the duration of work (hour/day etc.).

Piece rate 📦

Payment is based on the number of pieces produced.

Overtime rate ⏰

Rate for overtime work, irrespective of whether the normal rate is time-based or piece-based.

5

Committees & Boards

Who studies, advises and coordinates?

Wage Committee

Tripartite participation

Appropriate Government forms it with representatives from employer and employee sides. An independent person may be appointed as chairperson, as described in the text.

Advisory Board

Recommendations

Section 7: proposes recommendations/changes and advises the State and Central Governments on minimum-wage fixation.

Central Advisory Board

Coordination

Advises Central and State Governments on fixation/revision and coordinates the work of State Advisory Boards.

Composition

Balanced representation

The book lists employers, employees and independent persons. Employer and employee representation is equal; independent persons are limited to one-third, with an independent person as chair.

How does wage payment work?

Payment form, rate and workday

Wages in kind — Section 11 (as in the text)

Wages are stated to be paid in current coin/currency notes. Where payment wholly or partly in kind is customary, the appropriate government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, authorise such payment.

Time-based

Amount follows time worked or the time unit fixed.

Piece-based

Amount follows pieces produced.

Overtime

Separate overtime rate applies to overtime work.

Short workday

The supplied MCQ says a worker employed for less than the requisite normal working day is entitled to wages for a full normal working day (subject to the question’s stated context).

Enforcement & non-compliance

Inspection → claim/relief → consequence

Inspector checks
→
Worker may seek redress

Authority considers claim

Inspector

The text refers to inspector powers/duties to monitor employer adherence and prevent infringement.

Claims authority

Appropriate authorities are provided where employees seek relief for failure to pay wages.

Non-compliance

Failure to pay minimum wages is described as punishable; the supplied text mentions imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine up to ₹10,000 as its stated maximum.

Working hours

The text notes that violation of fixed working hours can also attract penal provisions.
